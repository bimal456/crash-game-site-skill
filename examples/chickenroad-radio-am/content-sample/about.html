How the pages are kept up to date

The process is simple and repeatable. There are no laboratory claims or secret data, and nothing is published unless it can be traced to the game itself or to a named source.

  1. Start with the provider. Official specs such as the declared RTP, modes and release date come from the InOut Games page, treated as the maker's claim.
  2. Check the info panel. Specs are checked against the in-game rules and info panel, which you can open yourself in the free demo.
  3. Cross-check databases. Lane counts and multiplier ranges are compared with established game databases, and differences are noted.
  4. Go to the regulator. Legal status and licensing rules come from regulators' own websites and registers.
  5. Re-date on update. Whenever a page is updated, the date at the top changes.

When a number depends on the operator, such as the RTP build, bet limits or the maximum win, the page says so and asks you to check the game's info panel at your casino. When a fact is uncertain, the page says it is uncertain.

Each page shows the date it was last updated. When the provider changes a game, when a regulator changes its rules or when a reader sends a correction that can be confirmed, the page is revised and that date changes. Pages about legal status and tax get the most frequent checks, because those details change more often than the game itself. The same method applies to every edition: a UK legal update gets re-checked against the UK Gambling Commission, an India update against the 2025 Act, and so on for each country covered.

What is never published: fake player comments, invented testimonials, made-up win screenshots, "live results", or claims that a strategy or app can predict the game. The pros and cons on the home page summarise documented facts, not personal opinions.
